About the fund

The Sunset Community Fund is a county-level fund that pays for after-school arts and mentoring in Franklin County, Ohio. It was founded in 2014 by a group of parents and retired teachers who wanted a single small-grant source that neighbourhood programs could reach without a development director. The fund makes grants totalling about $180,000 a year, spread across roughly twenty organizations, so the typical award is deliberately small and the paperwork is deliberately short. Every application is read by a volunteer review committee of local teachers and working artists, most of whom have run a youth program themselves and know what a realistic budget looks like. The committee meets twice a year, reads every complete application in full, and writes a short set of notes back to each applicant whether or not the request is funded.

What we fund

We fund after-school and summer arts programs for young people, including visual art, music, theatre, dance, creative writing, and media production. We fund mentoring programs that carry a real arts component, meaning the young person makes something and an adult with practice in that form works alongside them. We fund equipment for youth art rooms, such as kilns, sewing machines, drum kits, cameras, printers, and the storage that keeps them usable. We fund artist stipends, because a program that expects working artists to teach for free is a program that will not last a second year. We do not fund capital campaigns, building purchases, or renovations, we do not fund scholarships paid to individuals, and we do not fund galas, auctions, or other fundraising events, however well attended they are.

Eligibility

Applicants must be 501(c)(3) organizations serving young people ages 8 to 18 in Franklin County, Ohio. Organizations with an annual budget under $500,000 receive priority. Fiscal sponsorship is accepted with a signed letter from the sponsor.

Programs that operate outside Franklin County are not eligible, even when the applicant organization is headquartered inside the county, because the fund is answerable to donors who gave for this county's young people. For-profit entities are not eligible in any form, including a for-profit studio applying through a nonprofit partner that would pass the money straight through. Programs that charge participation fees above $50 per term are not eligible, because a fee above that line has been shown, in this county, to shut out the families the fund exists to reach.

Award size

Awards range from $5,000 to $15,000. Most first-time grantees receive between $5,000 and $8,000. Multi-year funding is not offered; prior grantees may reapply each cycle.

How to apply

Send a two-page narrative that says who the program serves, what the young people will make, and who will teach them. Send a one-page budget for the program, not for the whole organization, showing what this grant would pay for line by line. Send your most recent Form 990, or a signed fiscal sponsor letter if you are applying under a sponsor. Send two references, each a person outside your organization who has seen the program run, with a phone number and an email address for each. Send a program calendar with the start and end dates and the weekly meeting times, submit the whole package by email as a single PDF, and expect no interview stage, because the committee reads only what you send; questions are answered until October 8, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Do you accept late applications? No. The deadline of October 15, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. Eastern is the deadline, and an application that arrives at 5:01 p.m. is not read. The committee has a fixed number of volunteer hours and holds the line so that the organizations who met the date are not penalised for meeting it.

Can we be funded for part of what we asked for? Yes. Partial funding is common and is not a comment on the quality of the application. If the committee funds part of a request, the award letter will say which budget lines it covers, and you may spend it only on those lines unless you write to us first and we agree in writing to a change.

What reporting do you require? One report, one page, due June 30, 2027. Tell us how many young people took part, what they made, what the money actually paid for, and what you would do differently. We do not require receipts with the report, but keep them for a year in case we ask. A missing report makes an organization ineligible for the following cycle.

Can a school apply? Not directly. Public and charter schools are not eligible applicants for this fund, because the fund is meant to reach programs that sit outside the school budget. A school can take part in a funded program as a partner, and a nonprofit partner can apply on behalf of that partnership, name the school in the narrative, and hold the grant.

Do you consider equipment-only requests? Yes, up to $5,000. An equipment-only request should still explain the program the equipment serves, who will maintain the equipment, and where it will be stored between sessions. Requests for equipment that will live in a private home or a leased space the organization does not control are not funded.

Can we submit two proposals in one cycle? No. One organization, one proposal, one cycle. If two proposals arrive from the same organization, the committee reads the one that arrived first and returns the second unread. An organization applying under a fiscal sponsor counts as its own applicant, and the sponsor may sponsor more than one applicant.
